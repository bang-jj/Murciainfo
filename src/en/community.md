## 1. Your residence paperwork depends on your passport

{% grid %}
{% card "🇮🇪 Ireland (and other EU passports)" %}
You're an **EU citizen**: no visa and no TIE. For stays over 3 months, register within 3 months of arrival for the **Certificado de Registro de Ciudadano de la Unión** (form **EX-18**), which includes your NIE. Your **EHIC** covers state healthcare.
{% endcard %}
{% card "🇬🇧 United Kingdom" %}
Since Brexit, British students are **non-EU**: for an exchange longer than 90 days you need a **student visa** before you travel and a **TIE** after you arrive (see [Visa & TIE](visa.html)). UK students can also apply for a **student GHIC** for state healthcare in the EU — check the NHS website.
{% endcard %}
{% card "🇺🇸 🇨🇦 🇦🇺 🇳🇿 US, Canada, Australia, New Zealand" %}
Non-EU: apply for a **student visa** at the Spanish consulate in your country before you leave, then get your **TIE** in Murcia. Private health insurance covering your whole stay is usually required for the visa.
{% endcard %}
{% endgrid %}

{% alert "⚠️ The 90/180 rule", "warning" %}
Non-EU visitors can only spend **90 days in any 180** in the Schengen Area without a visa or residence card. Weekend trips before your visa starts or after it ends all count — plan your arrival and post-semester travel carefully.
{% endalert %}

## 2. Money and daily life

- **Cards work almost everywhere,** including contactless and Apple/Google Pay. Still keep a little cash for small bars, markets and tobacco shops.
- **Avoid foreign transaction fees:** many students use **Wise** or **Revolut** for euros instead of their home bank card. Always choose to pay in **euros** when a card machine offers your home currency.
- **Tipping isn't expected.** Rounding up or leaving a euro or two for good service is plenty — nothing like US tipping.
- **Spanish timetable:** lunch is around 14:00–16:00 and dinner rarely before 21:00. Many small shops close in the afternoon, and supermarkets close on Sundays.
- **Plugs:** Spain uses European type C/F plugs at 230 V. UK, US and Australian devices need an adapter.

## 3. Language and making friends

- **English isn't widely spoken in Murcia** outside the university — learning even basic Spanish makes daily life much easier. UCAM offers Spanish courses for exchange students (see [Courses](courses.html)).
- **Language exchanges (intercambios):** locals who want to practise English are often happy to swap for Spanish practice — a great way to make Spanish friends.
- **ESN events** at bars like Taboo Bar are where most international students meet (see [Attractions](attractions.html)).
- **English-language books** are hard to find (Casa del Libro has just one shelf), so bring a Kindle or use a library app.

## 4. Emergencies

- **Emergency number in Spain: 112** (police, ambulance, fire) — operators can often help in English.
- **Register with your embassy:** most English-speaking countries have their embassy in **Madrid**, and some have consulates closer to Murcia (for example in Alicante). Check your government's travel website for the nearest office and register your trip if your country offers it.
